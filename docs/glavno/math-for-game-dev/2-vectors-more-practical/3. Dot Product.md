# Dot Product equation

 I assume It is called dot, because we put dot (`·`) between two Direction Vectors explain this operation. 
 
$$
\begin{gather}
\vec{v}_{1} \cdot \vec{v_{2}} = |\lvert a\rvert| & |\lvert b\rvert| & \cos{\theta} 
\end{gather}
$$

Looking at equation above can also tell you that there is a two way of calculating dot product between two Direction Vectors. Look at the left side of the equation and look at the right side.

Next we will explore both ways of calculating dot product. And in that process we will also make metioned equation be more clear 


# Computing Dot Product

## `v1 · v2`  way

- scalar value is floating point number

$$
\begin{gather}
\vec{v}_{1} \cdot \vec{v_{2}}

$$

## `||v1|| * ||v2|| * cos(θ)`  way

By reading next equation you can say that you can calculate Dot Product of two Direction vectors by multiplying their magnitudes (||a|| and ||b||) together and multiplying  cosinus of the angle theta between these mentioned vector (`cos(θ)`) 
$$
\begin{gather}
|\lvert a\rvert| & |\lvert b\rvert| & \cos{\theta} 
\end{gather}
$$

`||a||` is magnitude/length of `a` vector, `||b||`  is magnitude/length og `b` vector

And `θ` angle is angle between a vector and b vector

So you can also calculate dot product  if you have angel between two vectors; you can multiply cosinus of the angle and  magnitudes/lengths of both vectors.

# Scalar Projection

The scalar projection of **v1 onto v2** is: how far v1 extends _in the direction of v2_, as a signed length

I'll try to explain this with examples

## Scalar Projection of one random vector onto another

> In game dev we always keep our vector direction normalized because it makes calculations simple and easy (but this time we are learning so it is ok, but keep that in mind)

We can also say scalar projection of non-normalized vectors.

We have two random vectors  v1 and v2 (with two different magnitudes)

Draw a line from the **tip (head) of v1** down to the **line containing v2**, such that this line is **perpendicular to v2**.

![[projector-projected.excalidraw|700]]

How do we calculate it

Let's start with equation for dot product
$$
\begin{gather}
\vec{v}_{1} \cdot \vec{v_{2}} = |\lvert a\rvert| & |\lvert b\rvert| & \cos{\theta} 
\end{gather}
$$

Devide entire equation with magnitude of the vector we are projection on, so in this case we are projecting on v2.

$$\frac{v_1 \cdot v_2}{|v_2|} = \frac{|v_1||v_2|\cos\theta}{|v_2|} = |v_1|\cos\theta$$

so

```ts
sclarProjection v1 onto v2 = || v1 ||  *  cos(θ)
```


or we can look it through pytagorean theorem to understand what actually we did

# Recap

**Dot Product**
- takes two vectors and returns sclar value
- $$
\begin{gather}
\vec{v}_{1} \cdot \vec{v_{2}} = |\lvert a\rvert| & |\lvert b\rvert| & \cos{\theta} 
\end{gather}
$$

- mesures similarity between two vectors; how closely they align in direction
- if vectors are orthogonal, it is always 0
- easy to calculate: `a · b = a.x * b.x + a.y * b.y`

**Scalar Projection**
- how far v1 extends _in the direction of v2_, as a signed length
- $$\frac{v_1 \cdot v_2}{|v_2|} = \frac{|v_1||v_2|\cos\theta}{|v_2|} = |v_1|\cos\theta$$
- for unit vectors always same as dot product (which is important one since we are deling mostly with unit vectors in game dev) 
```ts
	  sclar_projection = v1 · v2 = cos( θ )
```
	beacuse magnitudes are 1 in case of unit vectors

- Relating the angle between two vectors
- Projectionf one vector onto another
