## Summary

So what that means is that if you multiply by `i` on the left and then the inverse negative `i` on the right like this, `i` times quaternion times negative `i`,
![[2026-10-03_21-25.png]]
you end up with no change at all on `wx` and two rotations on `yz`. And if you really wanted to, you could work through it and you'd find the same thing would happen if you did all of that but rotating on another axis.

Like let's say that you multiply by `j` or by `k`. 

So the upshot of all of this is that basically by multiplying on the left by the rotation and then on the right by the inverse rotation, you apply the rotation twice and cancel out any rotation in that fourth dimension, the `w` dimension.

So then you just define your quaternion as half the rotation you want instead.
And that's why you use `cos(θ/2) and sin(θ/2).

The quaternion is only built with half of the angle of rotation because when you actually apply it, it gets applied twice. So the rotation is in effect doubled when you use it.
