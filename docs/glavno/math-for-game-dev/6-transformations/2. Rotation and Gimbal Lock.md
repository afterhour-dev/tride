# Conventions

Not everyone follows therse but understanding what convention your specific game environment uses is good to know. You may or may not follow that rule.
![[2026-10-01_21-25.png]]
especially if you are working in internal studio code. They may copy/past it from somewhere, and only roughly checked that it worked rotating things but not neccesseraly checked or even know which way should normaly rotate.

But by understanding the basic principals, yo ushould be able to adapt to what ever choice they made.

## Advantages

- Simple to represent a combined rotation
- Simply by multiplying them together, the idividual rotation matrices, you can get the final rotation matrix that represents the total rotation
- They are straight forward to understand giving that you already know how matrices, basis vectors, and visualization of them all work.
- Compatible with standard transforms. So rotation can be included as part of the single: scale, rotation and translation matrix.

## Disadvantages

- Complexity around interpolation. You can't linearly interpolate, or in other words, you can't blend between matrix rotations easily. Generarly you need to switch out representation to something else like quaternions in order to interpolate.
- Rotation matrices are offten formed from Euler angles, which we are about to cover. And Euler suffers from a problem called Gimbal lock which we will also touch.
