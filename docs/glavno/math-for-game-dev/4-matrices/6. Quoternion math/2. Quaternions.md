The first step is that you kind of promote the vector into a quaternion by assigning the `ijk` parts of the quaternion with the `xyz` components from the vector.

You're gonna be able to look around, so rotation on x, z or around the y axis, and you're gonna be able to look up and down, so rotation on y z or around the x axis. Keeping everything in Euler angles in this case would make my life significantly simpler.

For matrices these are basically required in game code since interaction with a graphics system will need matrices.

I also like to use them directly in code when I want to orient something easily to look at something else, for example a camera. I can orient it to look at another object trivially by just setting the basis vectors.

For quaternions these will be useful in any situation where you need full freedom of rotation on any axis. Think of a controller for a spaceship or something to that effect. Also anything that needs to take advantage of something like SLURP will need an internal representation in quaternions.

An easy example being blending between skeletal animations.

I'd avoid using them in any situation that's exposed directly to editing. Interfaces and such are always better off having simple intuitive representations such as Euler angles. You can always convert to a quaternion later after loading the data.
