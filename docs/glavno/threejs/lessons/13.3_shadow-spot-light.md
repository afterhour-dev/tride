## Concept

After tuning directional shadows in [[13.1_shadow-map-optimization-part-one]] and [[13.2_shadow-map-optimization-part-two]], this lesson turns to the **spot light** and what shadow-specific options it adds. The spotlight's shadow camera is a **`PerspectiveCamera`** (a cone), unlike the directional light's orthographic box — so the shadow tuning knobs change shape: no more `top/right/bottom/left`, instead `fov`, `near`, `far`, and a new `focus` property.

Much of what was true for directionals stays true here: the same `mapSize` budget and the same `renderer.shadowMap.type` algorithm apply. The spotlight also has its own twist on the "amplitude" knob, and the app runs in `three/webgpu` (`WebGPURenderer`), which surfaces one particularly nasty cache bug when you move `spotLight.target`.

### mapSize — same technique as the directional light

The resolution budget works identically:

```ts
spotLight.shadow.mapSize.width = 1024;
spotLight.shadow.mapSize.height = 1024;
```

Keep the two dimensions equal, and prefer powers of two — this is the one and same [LightShadow.mapSize](https://threejs.org/docs/#api/en/lights/LightShadow.mapSize) property. Bigger texture = sharper shadows, more memory.

### The amplitude knob — `focus`, not `fov`

Here is the headline of this lesson, and it costs people hours.

A `SpotLightShadow`'s camera has a `fov` property, and it is very tempting to GUI-slider it. **It will not stick.** Every frame, before the shadow map renders, `SpotLightShadow.updateMatrices()` recalculates and **overwrites** the camera's `fov`:

$$ fov = \text{RAD2DEG} \cdot 2 \cdot \text{light.angle} \cdot \text{light.shadow.focus} $$

So any `fov` you set by hand (e.g. through lil-gui) is clobbered on the very next frame — this is known three.js behavior, **not a bug in your code**. The *actual* knob for the size of the shadow frustum (the "amplitude" for a cone) is `spotLight.shadow.focus`:

```ts
spotLight.shadow.focus = 1; // default — matches the light's angle exactly
```

- `focus: 1` → the shadow camera sees exactly the light's cone (`angle`).
- `focus < 1` (e.g. `0.5`) → **narrower** frustum than the cone. Tighter fit around the lit area, less shadow-map resolution wasted on parts of the cone that don't hit anything.
- `focus > 1` → wider frustum than the cone — you can see the shadow edge start to rasterize with lower detail.

Think of `focus` as a percentage of the spotlight's field of view. You never need to call `updateProjectionMatrix()` for it — three.js recomputes the camera itself every frame from `focus`.

### near and far — only `near` is yours

The same `updateMatrices()` pass also manages `far`:

$$ far = \text{light.distance} \;\; || \;\; \text{camera.far} $$

So the shadow camera's far plane **tracks `spotLight.distance`** automatically. Setting `spotLight.shadow.camera.far` by hand (worse, setting it to `0`) does nothing either. To change the shadow's far range, change `spotLight.distance`, not the shadow camera. This behaviour is documented directly on `LightShadow` — if the light sets `distance`, the far clipping plane follows it; otherwise it defaults to `500`.

`near`, however, is **genuinely not auto-managed** — `updateMatrices()` only touches `fov`, `aspect`, and `far`. A `near` slider is a real, persistent control: raise it and the shadow visibly crops/becomes smaller, exactly as expected.

### The target — where the shadow camera points

A spotlight aims via an `Object3D` target, and so does its shadow camera: every frame, `updateMatrices()` does a `lookAt()` from `spotLight.position` toward `spotLight.target`'s world position. That only works if the target is in the scene graph:

```ts
scene.add(spotLight.target);
```

This is easy to forget — if the target isn't added to the scene, moving it never updates `matrixWorld`, and the light (and its shadow) ignore it.

### A real `three/webgpu` bug: shadows ignore `target` movement

In this app, moving `spotLight.position` reorients the drop shadow as expected, and the shadow already updates when you move the *camera*... but **moving `spotLight.target` does nothing to the shadow** (the helper still moves — showing the camera *thinks* it has reoriented). In normal `WebGLRenderer` this works; this specific mismatch is a **known WebGPU-backend caching bug**: the node-based lighting/shadow system caches light data more aggressively than WebGL and doesn't detect that the target-driven orientation changed. Reported three.js issues cover exactly this class of light-cache staleness on the WebGPU path.

Workarounds to try (in increasing order of confidence):
1. Confirm it's WebGPU-specific — temporarily swap to `THREE.WebGLRenderer` with the same scene; if `target` movement works there, the backend is the culprit.
2. Update three.js — this area has seen active fixes across recent releases; you may be on a partially-patched version.
3. Force the matrix update each frame in your `tick()`:

```ts
spotLight.target.updateMatrixWorld(true);
```

Cheap to test, but won't help if the staleness is in shader/uniform caching rather than matrices.
4. If it persists, file a minimal repro on the [three.js issues](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues) — the maintainers fix these piecemeal, and a clean repro helps.

### Mixing shadows doesn't look great

The scene has both a directional and a spot light casting shadows. Combined, the drop shadows get muddy — matching the different `shadowMap.type` filters and map sizes of two overlapping projections doesn't produce a clean result. Realistically, prefer one dominant shadow-casting light and keep the others for fill/rim without shadows.

### `decay`, `intensity`, `angle` — a quick reminder

The spotlight shadow quality is bounded by the light's **`angle`** (cone half-width — wider cone with a fixed `mapSize` means blurrier shadow rasterization, hence the appeal of `focus` to recover detail) and its **`distance`** (drives the shadow far plane). `intensity` here is in **candelas** (not a `0..1` slider — the app uses `0.4 * Math.PI` with `decay = 0` to mimic a legacy light; see [[12.6_spot-light]] for the full rundown).

## Code

in repo - `apps/13_3_shadow_spot_light`

```ts
import * as THREE from 'three/webgpu';

const renderer = new THREE.WebGPURenderer({ canvas });
await renderer.init();
renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true;
renderer.shadowMap.type = THREE.PCFSoftShadowMap;

const scene = new THREE.Scene();
const camera = new THREE.PerspectiveCamera(75, innerWidth / innerHeight, 0.1, 100);
camera.position.set(2, 1, 1);

// Spot light with shadow
const spotLight = new THREE.SpotLight(0xffffff);
spotLight.intensity = Math.PI;     // candelas, not 0..1
spotLight.distance = 10;           // drives the shadow camera's far plane
spotLight.decay = 0;
spotLight.angle = Math.PI * 0.3;
spotLight.position.set(0, 2, 2);
spotLight.castShadow = true;       // don't forget!

spotLight.shadow.mapSize.width = 1024;
spotLight.shadow.mapSize.height = 1024;
spotLight.shadow.camera.near = 1;  // the one shadow-camera knob that sticks
spotLight.shadow.focus = 1;        // the real "amplitude" control (× light.angle)

scene.add(spotLight);
scene.add(spotLight.target);       // REQUIRED, or target moves are ignored

// Objects
const material = new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial({ roughness: 0.7 });
const sphere = new THREE.Mesh(new THREE.SphereGeometry(0.5, 32, 32), material);
sphere.castShadow = true;
const floor = new THREE.Mesh(new THREE.PlaneGeometry(5, 5), material);
floor.receiveShadow = true;
floor.rotation.x = -Math.PI / 2;
floor.position.y = -0.5;
scene.add(sphere, floor);

// Helpers must be refreshed every frame
const spotHelper = new THREE.SpotLightHelper(spotLight);
const shadowHelper = new THREE.CameraHelper(spotLight.shadow.camera);
scene.add(spotHelper, shadowHelper);

renderer.setAnimationLoop(() => {
  spotHelper.update();
  shadowHelper.update();
  renderer.render(scene, camera);
});
```

## Gotchas

- **`spotLight.shadow.camera.fov` does nothing.** Hand-set values are overwritten every frame by `fov = RAD2DEG * 2 * light.angle * light.shadow.focus`. The GUI in this app ships the slider disabled with an explanation. Use `spotLight.shadow.focus` instead.
- **`spotLight.shadow.camera.far` does nothing too.** It's recomputed every frame as `light.distance || camera.far`. Change `spotLight.distance` instead — and `far = 0` isn't even a valid clipping plane, so it'd be clamped anyway.
- **`near` is the only trusted shadow-camera knob.** It's not auto-managed, so raising it crops the shadow noticeably — the expected behaviour for an actual persistent property.
- **Don't forget `scene.add(spotLight.target)`.** Without the target in the scene graph, its `matrixWorld` never updates and neither the light nor its shadow aim at it.
- **Moving `spotLight.target` may not move the shadow on `three/webgpu`.** A known caching bug in the WebGPU backend; `position` updates propagate but target-driven `lookAt` orientation doesn't. Verify against `WebGLRenderer`, update three, or force `spotLight.target.updateMatrixWorld(true)` in `tick()`.
- **Mixing several `castShadow` lights looks muddy.** Different filters/map sizes/amplitudes on overlapping shadows don't compose cleanly. Prefer one dominant shadow-casting light.
- **Keep `shadow.mapSize.width` and `.height` equal** (powers of two), same as the directional case.
- **`radius` (blur) still doesn't compose with `PCFSoftShadowMap`** — carries over from [[13.2_shadow-map-optimization-part-two]]; the spotlight `spotLight.shadow.radius` won't blur under the default soft algorithm.
- **Wider `angle` with a fixed `mapSize` = blurrier shadow detail.** There's no orthographic "box to tighten" here — tighten via `focus` instead.

## Revisit

- **The `three/webgpu` target-shadow bug.** Document whether a three.js update fixes it; if not, file/upvote a minimal repro. Actually triage between the `updateMatrixWorld(true)` workaround and a renderer-version bump.
- **Why `updateMatrices()` owns `fov`/`far`.** Read `SpotLightShadow` source to internalize which shadow-camera properties are recomputed vs. user-persistent — it pins down *why* the GUIs use `focus` and `distance`.
- **Mixing shadows quality.** Revisit whether layered `castShadow` lights can be made to look good (per-light `mapSize`/`focus`/algorithm tuning), or whether the practical rule stays "one shadow light".
- **`focus` vs `angle` precision trade-off.** Explore `focus: 0.5` on a narrow cone with a big `mapSize` — where does the shadow start looking noticeably crisper, and where does it clip?
- **Outdated GUI names in the app:** the `far` slider in `13_3` shines through a `.disable()` but still says `'spotLight.shadow.camera.far'` while the `fov` slider is mislabeled `'spotLight.shadow.camera.fov'` only by intent — both are documented here as overridden properties, and the *working* twins (`distance`, `focus`) are in the open `Spot Light` / shadow-tweaks folders.

## Outdated

approach is valid

Spot light shadows map size, focus, near/far and the `focus`/`distance`-driven overrides are identical under WebGL and WebGPU. **This app already runs on `three/webgpu` + `WebGPURenderer` (WebGPU path) ⚡** — the modern rendering route. The one caveat is the actively-patched WebGPU light/*shadow caching bug described in the Concept; keep three.js updated and treat `target` movement under the WebGPU backend with suspicion until confirmed fixed in your version.
